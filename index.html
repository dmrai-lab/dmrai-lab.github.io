Open Science · Diffusion MRI · Microstructure Imaging

Physics is
the specification.
Open code is how we check it.

Diffusion MRI can see inside living tissue without a scalpel — inferring axon diameters, membrane densities, soma permeability from the motion of water molecules. dmrai-lab is building open, validated, reproducible infrastructure for this work: a Monte-Carlo engine whose walks are stored once and replayed for any acquisition, a reader that needs only NumPy, the closed forms they share, and the fitting, design and tractography tools that check against them, free for the whole field to use, extend, and check.

Active · flagship
dmipy3.0.0
The one thing you install. A single pip install dmipy pulls dmipy-sim, dmipy-replay, dmipy-fit and dmipy-design, with dmipy-analytic under them, in a known-good, validated combination, and pip install "dmipy[tract]" adds dmipy-tract; the cross-package benchmarks check them against each other, and the documentation is dmipy.org. The successor to the original 2019 dmipy toolbox, rebuilt in the open. Code: AGPL-3.0 or commercial

dmipy-analytic3.0.0
The closed and semi-closed forms of the diffusion-MRI signal for special cases: free and restricted diffusion, exchange, relaxation, coherence pathways, orientation distributions and the fields of simple shapes, each with the assumptions it holds under and the source it comes from. NumPy definitions with JAX twins: the one copy the other packages import, and the forms a replay pack is checked against. Apache-2.0
closed forms NumPy · JAX cited sources validation
Active
dmipy-replay0.2.0
The reader of replay packs: NumPy only, no walk, no GPU. It owns the acquisition object every package shares, reads packs from the Hugging Face Hub by ranged requests, replays any acquisition against them, by the phase or through the actual pulses (vector Bloch), and states what a replay will read before it reads. Every pack states the envelope it is certified for (a band, a gradient amplitude, field and tissue bounds), and a request outside it is refused by name. Apache-2.0
NumPy only Hub client vector Bloch certified envelope
Active
dmipy-sim3.0.0
The Monte-Carlo replay engine. Spins walk once through an explicit tissue substrate (axon bundles, packed cylinders and spheres, meshes, DiSCo strands) with surface relaxivity, membrane permeability, magnetization transfer and susceptibility fields in the walk. The walk is stored as a certified replay pack that answers any gradient waveform, RF schedule, tissue value and orientation without another simulation. JAX, on CPU or GPU. AGPL-3.0 or commercial
Monte Carlo replay packs wall physics JAX GPU
Active
dmipy-fit3.0.0
JAX-native fitting across the full landscape of compartment models, vmap-ed across voxels on GPU. T2, T1 and surface relaxivity as composable factors, exact matrix-method restricted-diffusion compartments, and replay compartments that fit through a stored Monte-Carlo walk. Batched CSD and DTI on the device, with explicit scope boundaries characterized against Monte-Carlo ground truth. AGPL-3.0 or commercial
JAX GPU matrix method replay compartments T2 · T1 · surface relaxivity CSD
Active
dmipy-design3.0.0
Deliverable acquisition design under real scanner limits: gradient waveforms for any b-tensor shape (LTE, PTE, STE, OGSE, stimulated echo), B1-robust refocusing pulses, and waveforms designed through replay packs to tell substrates apart. It exports scanner-runnable Pulseq sequences. AGPL-3.0 or commercial
waveform design scanner limits refocusing RF Pulseq export

dmipy-tract0.1.0
Streamline tractography on fibre-orientation fields, batched on the device. One FOD field, one tracker (probabilistic and deterministic rules, JAX or PyTorch), one tractogram written as .tck. It follows dipy's LocalTracking conventions, and a streamline depends on the key, its seed and the field alone. AGPL-3.0 or commercial
FOD tracking JAX · PyTorch reproducible streams

SubstrateCommons
A public organisation on the Hugging Face Hub, open to anyone with no login: replay packs of canonical pores (planes, cylinders and spheres), a permeable slab, CACTUS axons, the DiSCo phantom and more. Each pack is certified for a stated envelope, not for a list of protocols, and is read with dmipy-replay, from a file or by range. Replay packs: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 or commercial

DiSCo Space
The DiSCo connectivity phantom replayed in the browser on a shared GPU: pick an acquisition (DiSCo's own, clinical, research, Connectome 2.0, OGSE), replay every voxel from the stored walk, reconstruct and track. The packs come from SubstrateCommons; the code is dmipy-sim, dmipy-fit and dmipy-tract. Source: disco-space.

Vision

The goal is not a faster fitting tool — it is an open, validated foundation the whole field can build on together.

The name is the thesis in miniature: dmrai is diffusion MRI + AI. Diffusion, because water diffusion is the mechanism beneath every quantity we estimate — relaxation, susceptibility, and exchange are all read out through how water moves; quantitative maps are what you get, water diffusion is how you get them. AI, because the work is built by and with AI agents.

dmrai-lab is the revival and extension of dmipy, originally published in Frontiers in Neuroinformatics (2019). We are rebuilding it in the open, around a Monte Carlo replay engine that provides the ground truth, a walk stored once that answers any acquisition, and the tools that check against it: a JAX fitting engine grounded in biophysical compartment models, an acquisition designer and a tractography tracker.

Our north star: a certified virtual MRI. Any tissue, any sequence a scanner can play, its signal computed from the physics of how water moves, with its error stated, on an ordinary computer and open for anyone to check. On that one foundation, every quantitative MRI method, whatever it reads (diffusion, relaxation, exchange, magnetisation transfer, susceptibility), can be designed, fitted and validated against ground truth instead of assumption.

The bottleneck was never intelligence. It was shared infrastructure — and that is finally something we can build together.

    About

    Dr. Rutger Fick

    dmrai-lab is one researcher working with an AI. Since the revival in April 2026, the work has been a collaboration between Rutger Fick and Claude, Anthropic's AI: Rutger as scientific director, setting the direction and making the decisions; Claude as lead researcher, writing and reviewing the code, designing and running the validations, and coordinating a small team of AI agents. Every result is held to the same rule: certified with its evidence, or not claimed.

    The researcher behind it has spent a decade publishing in diffusion MRI microstructure imaging — acquisition design, compartment modelling, Monte Carlo validation — and a parallel decade building AI products. dmrai-lab is where those two trajectories converge: the domain knowledge to know what the physics requires, and the engineering background to build the infrastructure that enforces it.
